Reading a game

  • The house shows every stone in play after the rock you're on, and the path of the rock that was thrown.
  • Step with the arrow keys or the arrows under the video. On a phone, tap ‹ and ›, or swipe across the house.
  • The video jumps to each rock as you step.
  • Change ends from the menu at the top. On a phone, tap the rock's name above the house.
  • A "?" is a rock the computer missed.
  • Report gives the score by end and each team's thinking time.

How good is it?

Close, not perfect. Stones in the house are placed from the overhead cameras. Guards are placed from the end cameras, which is harder: some are a foot or more out. Shot types are the computer's best guess.

Nothing here has been corrected by hand.

Where it comes from

FifthSense is built on curling_stats, the charting software Will Dimmit wrote for Granite Curling Club in Seattle and made public. A Rochester Curling Club member adapted it to the club's streams. This isn't an official club site.
